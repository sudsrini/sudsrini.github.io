## Project context

My integration work included the refurbishment of MB-1, The Boring Company's first rock tunnel boring machine, and the VC-6 Vertical Cassette assembly.

On MB-1, my scope covered ventilation, low-pressure fluid systems (LPF), and the tow beam. On VC-6, I was solely responsible for head-section and washbox integration.

<h2 id="interfaces">Build preparation and integration across both projects</h2>

### Prioritizing critical work

I identified critical-path tasks within my assigned subsystems and prioritized the parts, decisions, and corrections needed to move those tasks forward. I communicated across design, production, build, and operations to surface blockers and coordinate the work required for upcoming assembly stages.

### Preparing the work

Across MB-1 and VC-6, I prepared upcoming work by checking that kits contained the correct components and that the required drawings, instructions, and tooling were available. I checked part readiness before assembly and worked through missing or mismatched parts with the relevant teams.

### Checking fit and resolving discrepancies

For the parts I built, I reviewed available drawings against the physical hardware and checked that components fitted together as intended. When they did not, I compared the parts with the drawings, clarified the intended assembly, and coordinated next steps with design engineers and technicians. This included documenting discrepancies, resolving questions about the drawings, and coordinating corrections so the build could continue.

For an impact-bed assembly, the drawing did not clearly define the reference point for measuring the weld location, leading to a misunderstanding during the build. I helped clarify the intended measurement reference, document the correction through drawing redlines, and coordinate the correction with the team.

<h2 id="mb1">MB-1 refurbishment: designing around the installed hardware</h2>

I was responsible for integration of three MB-1 subsystems: the ventilation system, low-pressure fluid systems (LPF), and the tow beam.

### Constraints

Refurbishment meant working with an existing machine, incomplete CAD and drawings, and discrepancies between expected and available parts. Routing requirements could also change during the build. The integration work required decisions at the machine, with the actual components in view.

{% include case-figure.html src="/assets/case-studies/mb1-machine.jpg" caption="MB-1 main beam assembly, providing context for the refurbishment and integration work." %}

### My contribution

I worked with hydraulic technicians and design engineers to determine mounting positions and hose routes in situ. I selected available fittings to achieve the required connection direction and assembled them, checking connection compatibility, access, and clearance.

This involved adapting routes to the installed equipment and available inventory as requirements changed. My scope was the physical integration and resolution of subsystem interfaces during refurbishment.
